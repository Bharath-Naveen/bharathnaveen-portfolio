Overview01

The University of Arizona runs an analytics platform that compares credits earned against expected pacing and classifies each student as On Track, Behind, or Ahead, so advisors can spot patterns across a whole cohort. That system already existed when I joined.

Our student team was asked to restructure it and prototype a new approach, so that data science and machine learning work could be added to the pipeline later. We did not build that data science work ourselves. Our job was the bedrock it could be built on. I studied the existing architecture from its design documentation, then built and tested one piece of the new backend.

My part03

I worked on this in spring 2026 as a student contributor on a University of Arizona Vertically Integrated Projects (VIP) team. VIP is a university program in which faculty-led teams of undergraduate and graduate students work on long-term, multidisciplinary projects. Our team was led by Kristina Manasil, on a project overseen by Greg Heileman. My hands-on work was the backend prototype shown above: an AWS Lambda function, built and deployed in the AWS console, that takes read and write requests as JSON, a new DynamoDB table for the current plan, and version history in S3. Every plan is stored, and revert returns to the previously approved one, meaning the last plan the school cleared for credits and curriculum fit.

What worked: the function ran behind its own URL against the new table, kept every plan version in S3, and reverted to the previously approved plan. What was left: connecting the front end, and deciding how the prototype would fit into or replace parts of the existing system. It never touched the live database and was not released to production.

Student privacy: the student data used in this work was anonymized in line with FERPA, and I completed the university's FERPA training before working on the project.
